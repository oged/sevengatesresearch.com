# Short form

Notes, briefs, emails, memos, decision papers, handovers, LinkedIn or social posts, cover letters, summaries and replies. Short form compresses by removing points, never by removing grammar.

## 1. Universal rules

- The answer, decision or request is in the first sentence. The subject line states the action and topic.
- One job per paragraph. Short paragraphs. Bullets only for genuinely parallel items.
- Name who acts, what they do and by when.
- Include only the evidence the decision needs.
- Do not restate the request, the background the reader has, or the original email.
- No courtesy padding in documents; brief courtesy in the email cover line is fine.
- No closing summary and no offer of further help.
- Keep the small words. Meet a word limit by dropping a point, never by stacking nouns or deleting "that".

## 2. Email

Default 3 to 8 sentences and about 120 words, unless a table or a decision record is needed.

1. **Position**: one sentence that decides, confirms, clarifies, assigns, defers or escalates.
2. **Reason**: two to four reasons, MECE, each with its evidence.
3. **Action**: who, what, when, and what is still to be confirmed and by whom.

Tone: concise, direct, calm, decisive, respectful. Succinct without being stiff. No "kindly", "please be advised", "please do the needful", "I hope you are well" in operational mail. Never invent facts, dates, capacities, tags, approvals or conclusions; where uncertain, say what must be confirmed and by whom.

Versions on request: direct, diplomatic, executive-concise. They must differ in substance, not in synonyms.

## 3. Memo and decision paper

Decision required, by whom and by when; recommendation; options side by side on the same criteria; constraint and dependency; approval route; risks beside the claims they qualify; actions on approval with owners. Two pages unless the decision needs more. Lead each section with its conclusion.

## 4. Report summary and handover

- **Executive summary**: the answer first, then the three to five findings that carry it, then the decision. SCQA in four sentences at most if a frame is needed.
- **Handover**: status by system or workstream; open actions with owners; live decisions and limits in force; watch items; meetings and deadlines in the coming period; contacts. Same structure every time so the reader can scan it.

## 5. Seven Gates short note and Daily Brief

- Kicker, headline, deck, a compact opening, the judgement, two to four analytical sections, the implication, sources and disclaimer where required.
- A note on a listed stock still carries the five-year chart.
- The Brief follows `modules/seven-gates.md` section 10. Each item says what happened, why it matters for Nigerian prices, positions or policy, and what the market may be missing, with dated primary sources.
- Dry humour: two lines per brief at most, and only lines that carry a fact.
- The closing watch-list names signals and thresholds.

## 6. Posts (LinkedIn, X, newsletters)

- One idea per post. The first line carries the fact or the claim, not a hook formula.
- No engagement bait: no "Here's what nobody tells you", no "Let that sink in", no numbered "lessons" that are three restatements of one point, no one-word lines for drama.
- A number, a date and a source beat an adjective.
- A post that summarises a Seven Gates piece links to it and uses none of the piece's memorable lines verbatim more than once.
- Hashtags sparingly and literal.

### Threads promoting a Seven Gates piece

As delivered for Gun to Head at 252,000 in September 2026: three threads with different angles (a story hook, a data thread, a macro thread); every post under 280 characters; every figure taken from the article; no em dashes; the last post carries the link and "Not a recommendation"; attach the hero or the 1200x630 social card (export SVG figures to PNG); add the holdings disclosure when the narrator holds a stock mentioned ("Disclosure: I own GTCO and MTNN").

## 6a. Technical file note

An operator's file-note format, still a good default for a single engineering decision: a header table (To, From, Date, Office, Subject with the change reference); Introduction (what is proposed and that the note recommends an option); Background; one analysis section per hazard or issue; a worst-case calculation with its inputs and assumptions listed; codes cited by section; an options table (number, description, advantage, disadvantage), always including "do nothing"; a conclusion that recommends a numbered option; "END OF FILE NOTE". Captions as "Figure n:" and "Table n:".

## 6b. Status dashboards and reports

From a standard programme dashboard template: never leave a field blank (write N/A, because a blank reads as forgotten); avoid acronyms; dates in one format throughout; status legend On track, At risk, Off track, Not started, Complete, each with its word; a trend marker (improving, no change, declining); issues and risks each with a mitigation and an owner; a workstream table with original end, revised end, revision count, status and commentary. Estimates carry low, expected and high cases with the method stated.

## 7. Summaries of long material

- Preserve every number, unit and name exactly.
- Say what the source shows, what it claims and what it leaves out, in that order.
- Do not upgrade hedged findings or drop the conditions behind them.
- State the source and its date at the top.

## 8. Short-form checks

- Could the reader act after the first two sentences?
- Is every action owned and dated?
- Is anything said twice?
- Is there a sentence that does not help the reader understand, decide, approve or act? Remove it.
- Does it pass `core/02-human-prose.md` sections 2 to 4?
