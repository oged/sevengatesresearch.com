# Template: neutral and business development for others

For decks and documents issued for a third party (a client, a partner, a start-up, a friend's company), for business development where no house brand applies, and for anything where the user has not named a brand.

## 1. Choosing the look

1. If the client supplies a template, logo or brand guide, use it. Ask for it once before building.
2. If the client has a public brand (website, annual report), take its primary colour, one accent and its typeface family from those sources and confirm with the user.
3. Otherwise use the neutral default below.

Never put any house's marks (Seven Gates, an employer or a consulting firm) on third-party material unless the user says the house is issuing it.

## 2. Neutral default

- White ground `#FFFFFF`; charcoal `#2B2F33` for text and structure; one cool accent `#2C6E8F` used only where it carries meaning; light grey `#F2F2F2` panels; mid grey `#8B8F90` for labels and footers.
- Arial or the client's typeface. Never cream or beige by default; never a dark cover by default.
- Status colours only where status is the point: green `#2E8540`, amber `#D99A1E` (dark text), red `#B42318`, each with its word.
- Properties: author as the user specifies; company as the issuing party.

## 3. Business development deck spine

1. The problem the audience has, in their numbers.
2. What changes if it is solved (value at stake, with basis).
3. The offer: what exactly is proposed, scope and boundaries.
4. Proof: track record (attributed correctly), method, references, pilots.
5. Economics: price basis, savings or returns, assumptions labelled.
6. Risks and how they are carried.
7. Plan: phases, timeline, who does what.
8. The ask: decision, date, next step.

## 4. Rules for writing about someone else's business

- Use their terms, units and names exactly; confirm anything ambiguous.
- No claims about their performance that their own documents do not support.
- Track record from employment is shown as individual experience, never as the issuer's corporate history.
- Fees and values only as the user supplies them.
- Confidential information from one client never appears in another client's material.
