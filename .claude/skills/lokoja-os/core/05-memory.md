# Memory: the repetition engine

A house with a memory should not sound as though one template is being refilled. This file holds the permanent register (what is retired for good), the families and tics to rest, the rotation rules and the ledger protocol. The live, growing record for Seven Gates is `docs/repetition-ledger.md` in the website repository; this file is the rule and the permanent core, valid in every room.

## 1. How retirement works

- Retirement applies to the construction, scene and metaphor family, not only the wording. Changing the noun, city, drink, vehicle or celebrity does not make the device new.
- Retirement is permanent until an editor restores a construction in writing. Ageing out of a rolling window does not restore it.
- Retirement is editorial memory, not a ban on the concept. Explain the same mechanism directly when it is still relevant.
- Technical vocabulary (distinguish, market, settlement, custody, conversion, ratio, base, moat) stays available when it is the clearest term.
- A line should arrive, perform and retire.

### Retired for new copy (clean existing instances on next touch)

- "It does not establish X" or "That does not prove X" as a routine closing beat.
- "X would matter more than Y" as the watch-list formula.
- "X matters more than the headline, total or threshold".
- "The useful question is..."
- "The figures differ, but the direction is clear."
- "That does not make X Y" as a reflexive caveat.

### Rejected terms in technical rooms

These were rejected in operations work and are banned in every technical document: "holds back" or "held back" for deferral (write "deferral" or "potential deferral"); "when it binds" (write "constrains from" with the date); "water sets the limit"; "path forward"; "taken" for processed oil; "constrained oil" (write "oil production forecast"); "water handled" (write "water production forecast"); "kbd" (write mbd, mbwd, mmb).

## 4. Rotation rules

- **Rolling window**: the last 20 published pieces in the house. Openings, shapes, hero concepts, closing cadences, cultural guests and metaphor families are checked against it.
- **Permanent**: quotations, proverbs, retired families and article-linked signatures stay blocked whatever the window says, unless the new piece is specifically about that quotation, person or event.
- **Opening**: do not repeat the entry route of either of the last two pieces.
- **Structural devices** (interchapter, block quotation, remembered-experience opening, callback close) are counted; a third use in the window needs a reason.
- **Phrase fingerprint**: flag exact or near-exact memorable phrases of roughly six words or more, recurring opening cadences, repeated sentence skeletons, the same metaphor family with new nouns, repeated anecdote architecture with new names, repeated closing cadences, and hero concepts tied to the same joke.
- Standard legal language, source labels, technical terms, company names and necessary financial vocabulary are exempt. Everything memorable is not.

## 5. The ledger protocol

### Fields (one entry per published piece, newest first)

Date and slug; type, mode and rating; entry route in one line; narrative shape and where the ending lands; hero concept; cultural guests; quotations with speaker and verification status; proverbs, scripture and historical episodes; social types; closing cadence; memorable phrases of six words or more; analogy and metaphor families; structural devices with counts; first-person holdings mentioned; word count and any accepted length outside the mode range; advisory items left as published, each with a plan to resolve.

### When it is consulted

Twice: when the signature is chosen, and against the **final text** before release. Search the final article for every guest, quotation and device the ledger shows as spent. A plan that says "removed" proves nothing about the text.

## 6. Ledger integrity (the October 2026 lesson)

Between 24 October and 3 November 2026 the Seven Gates ledger lost entries in four commits made by the daily publishing run: five research entries silently dropped while recording a brief (24 October); an overwrite to 196 lines (27 October); an overwrite from 962 to 339 lines that deleted the remaining research entries and the whole corrections record (29 October); and an overwrite to 22 lines (3 November). The 27 October and 3 November losses were repaired the next day; the 24 and 29 October losses were not. `tools/ledger_guard.py --history` finds all four.

Rules that follow:

1. A ledger, register or log only grows, unless the commit is a stated correction or retirement.
2. Before writing a shared memory file, read its current version from the source of truth (pull `main`), append, then compare entry and row counts with the previous version. Run `tools/ledger_guard.py`.
3. Never write a memory file from a cached, templated or truncated copy.
4. Automated publishers must use an append operation, never a whole-file write.
5. A loss found later is restored from version history in a dedicated commit that names what it restores.

## 7. Memory outside Seven Gates

- **Consulting clients**: keep a short per-client log of phrases, analogies and slide titles used in each issued document, so that the next document for the same client does not repeat them.
- **Academic**: the memory is the reference list and the argument map. Do not reuse a paragraph across papers without citation to the earlier paper; self-plagiarism rules apply.
- **The editor's corrections**: any phrase or construction the editor rejects is added to section 2 with the date and the room, the same day.
