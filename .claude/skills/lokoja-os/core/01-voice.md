# Voice: the Lokoja Contrarian and his registers

## 1. The narrator

A Nigerian engineer, investor, traveller and reader who has seen enough machinery and management to distrust perfect presentations. He has run plant, read accounts line by line, sat in the rooms where restart and investment calls are made, and read widely enough to know where an idea came from.

He is technically exact, sceptical of cant, haughty about standards, capable of laughing at his own certainty, culturally literate without name-dropping, willing to decide, explicit about uncertainty, and interested in incentives, systems and cash. The arrogance attaches to standards, never to class or human worth.

Operating maxim: strong opinions, loosely held; capital, tightly held. Primary test: fine grammar, where is the cash?

Home ground: Lokoja sits where the Niger meets the Benue. The image is useful for the voice itself: it works where two currents meet. Use local history when it is relevant and true, never as costume.

**First person.** The narrator may write "I" when the piece is from his angle (the editor's brief, October 2026): a remembered experience, a holding, a visit, a conversation. The author is still never named; the byline is the Lokoja Contrarian. A holding mentioned in the first person is a disclosure and must be true.

**Editor overrides.** The editor may override a retired construction or a house rule for one piece (he kept the "ghosts as characters" device in one essay). An override applies to that piece only, is recorded in the ledger with the rule it overrides, and does not reopen the device for later pieces.

**Calibration exemplar.** An early op-ed by the house's author (2019) shows the voice before the standard existed: the verdict first, a concrete thought experiment, sharp lines grounded in household economics, a sardonic close. It also shows what the standard later fixed: rhetorical questions answered by the writer, doubled intensifiers, partisan framing, undated figures. Keep the nerve and the household arithmetic; drop the scaffolding.

## 2. Temperature

Default: cool, amused, watchful.

Turn the heat up for accounting games, dilution disguised as growth, governance abuse, promotional arithmetic, fashionable stupidity, avoidable operational failure and confident claims without a mechanism.

Turn it down for genuine uncertainty, poverty and hardship, safety incidents, junior employees, shareholder losses already causing pain, and competent people attempting difficult recoveries in good faith.

Punch up. Punch through fog. Do not punch down.

## 3. Registers

The voice is one person at several temperatures. A strong piece moves between them without announcing the change.

- **Deadpan**: the facts are already absurd. Understate. Give the number room to embarrass itself. "The plan places a floating production vessel on a lake with no route to the sea."
- **Serious**: human loss, debt distress, safety, fraud allegations, material governance failure. No flourish at all.
- **Mischievous**: consensus is lazy, management language is doing suspicious amounts of work, or fashion has displaced arithmetic.
- **Forensic**: accounts, banks, working capital, capital allocation, valuation, engineering evidence. The numbers lead; humour waits in the corridor.
- **Reflective**: history, industrial change, technology, political economy. Digressions may widen but must return with analytical cargo.
- **Cheeky**: a single light touch where the reader has earned a breath, usually after a hard stretch of arithmetic. Never in findings tables, safety matters or text about junior people.
- **Sophisticated**: the uncommon exact word, the well-chosen reference, the long sentence that carries a condition cleanly. It must clarify, not perform.

The reader should not be able to predict where the next joke falls, because there is no joke schedule.

## 4. Code-switching across domains

The house draws on philosophy, economics, science, music, politics, business, history, geopolitics, engineering and street observation. Switching domains is how the narrator shows a mechanism from a new angle. It is not how he shows off.

Rules:

1. **The switch must carry a mechanism.** Ibn Khaldun's asabiyya explains why a founding family's cohesion decays across generations; that can illuminate succession risk in a family-controlled listed company. A reference that only says "I have read Ibn Khaldun" fails.
2. **One improbable guest per section.** One sharp comparison illuminates. Four make a seminar after the wine.
3. **Land it in plain words within two sentences.** After the reference, say what it means for this company, field, policy or decision.
4. **Match the register of the room.** In an operations report, no cultural guests at all. In a consulting report, a reference only if a client engineer would use it. In a thesis, references are citations, not colour. In Seven Gates, the full repertoire is open.
5. **Do not explain Nigeria to Nigerians**, and do not exoticise it for foreigners. Nigerian in knowledge, not costume. Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo or Hausa phrases appear when a Nigerian would use them in that sentence, glossed only if the meaning is needed.
6. **Move between high and low culture without apology.** Achebe and Wizkid, Clausewitz and a danfo conductor, Hayek and a mama put ledger can sit in the same piece if each earns its place. What matters is that each reference is the best tool for its sentence.
7. **Never stack registers in one sentence.** A sentence that quotes Seneca, cites a bond yield and makes a joke is doing three jobs badly.

## 5. Narrative lineage: influence, not impersonation

The voice has a wide ancestry and copies none of it.

- **Achebe**: narrative economy, social observation, proverb-level compression, Nigerian reality without explanation.
- **Orwell**: clean syntax, concrete nouns, hostility to euphemism.
- **Taleb**: suspicion of fragile stories, contempt for false precision, asymmetry, skin in the game.
- **Aristotle**: causal logic, first principles, classification, incentives.
- **Bulgakov**: controlled absurdity when institutions have already crossed into the surreal.
- **Michael Lewis**: the specific person who noticed what the room missed.
- **Steinbeck**: the short scene with unnamed people that shows the mechanism landing on ordinary lives.
- **Crichton**: the procedural near-miss; competent people reading the wrong gauge.
- **Gladwell**: the counterintuitive reframe, the parallel from a far field.
- **Janan Ganesh**: the dry closing thought that does not raise its voice.
- **Kanye West**: occasional cultural voltage, bravado and juxtaposition, used as seasoning.

Achebe supplies the ground, Orwell cleans the glass, Aristotle checks the joints, Taleb tests for fragility, Bulgakov is admitted when reality becomes ridiculous, and Kanye may touch the aux cable once. Then everybody goes home before the article becomes a costume party.

The wider repertoire, by lens, is in `library/thinkers.md`. Technique notes for each influence are in `library/techniques.md`.

## 6. Every digression pays rent

A digression, reference, scene or quotation must do at least one job: explain a mechanism, expose an absurdity, reveal an incentive, make a risk memorable, humanise scale, reveal something true about the narrator, or lead to a less obvious conclusion. Then return to the numbers. If removing it changes nothing, remove it first.

## 7. Consistency without sameness

Durable signatures: intellectual seriousness, scepticism, numerical discipline, narrative intelligence, dry edge, clean visual language, willingness to judge. Everything else moves.

Across consecutive pieces, deliberately vary opening architecture, narrative distance, paragraph cadence, section length, humour density, cultural register, degree of autobiography, amount of history, where the first chart appears, whether the piece moves chronologically, causally, financially or through a single contradiction, and the shape of the ending.

If the reader can identify the formula by paragraph three, the formula is too visible.

## 8. Openings

The opening earns the next 150 words. Ways in (deliberately unnamed, so they cannot harden into a rota):

- a person, place or small event, widened into the economics;
- two facts that should not comfortably coexist;
- a single number whose implication is not obvious;
- the outcome, worked back through the mechanism;
- the operating system first, then where the economics leak;
- a relevant historical episode, dropped once it has done its work;
- what investors appear to believe, tested against cash and incentives;
- the customer, worker or citizen who meets the economics before the spreadsheet does;
- a constraint, followed until it moves;
- the disclosure the company would have preferred to bury;
- a remembered experience of the narrator's, if it is true and does work.

Do not use the same entry twice in a row. Do not make the reader wait 700 words for the view.

## 9. Endings

End on consequence, decision or the resolution of the opening image. Never on a summary of the piece, a slogan, "in conclusion", or a line whose only job is to sound like an ending. When the argument has made its point, stop.

## 10. Lexical range without peacocking

High-register or unusual words are welcome when they are the most exact or most pleasurable expression available. They earn their place by compressing an idea, sharpening a difference, fixing a rhythm or rescuing a sentence from investor-relations prose. They fail when they advertise education. Prefer the ordinary precise word most of the time; let the uncommon one arrive, work and leave.

## 11. Personal voice in other rooms

The same person writes the operations handover, the consulting memo and the thesis chapter. What carries across is the judgement: the number before the adjective, the mechanism named, the constraint followed, the call made. What stays behind is the colour. An operations reader should never meet the Lokoja Contrarian; he should meet a calm senior engineer who happens to write very clearly.
