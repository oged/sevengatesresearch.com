# Technique library

Craft techniques learnt from writers the house admires. Each entry is a technique, not a voice to imitate: what it does, how to use it, how it fails, and where it was last used (from the ledger; "Check the ledger" means the last use has not been confirmed). The self-improvement loop (`learning/loop.md` section 4) adds entries when a new exemplar is studied.

| Technique | From | What it does | How to use it | Failure mode | Last used |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Proverb-level compression | Achebe | Puts a social truth in one concrete line | Use a real proverb only when it carries the mechanism; otherwise write a plain line with the same economy | Proverb as costume; explaining the proverb | dangote-refinery-ipo-elephant (Pidgin proverb) |
| Concrete noun over abstraction | Orwell | Makes the reader see the thing | Replace "challenges" with the thing that went wrong | Bare lists of nouns without verbs | Every piece |
| The procedural near-miss | Crichton | Builds tension from competent people reading the wrong gauge | Clock times, instruments, a sequence; then the consequence | Fake drama; invented times | Check the ledger |
| The interchapter | Steinbeck | Shows the mechanism landing on ordinary lives | 100 to 150 words, unnamed people, present tense, labelled ILLUSTRATION | Sentimentality; third use in the window without a reason | chellarams (Isolo yard) |
| The person who noticed | Michael Lewis | Puts a face on an insight | Find the real analyst, engineer or trader in the record; quote what is documented | Inventing the person or their words | Check the ledger |
| The far-field reframe | Gladwell | Explains a mechanism through another domain | One parallel, landed in plain words within two sentences | Contested findings presented as law; more than one per section | chellarams (Outliers on meaningful work) |
| The quiet closing thought | Janan Ganesh | Ends a section without raising the voice | One per section at most, carrying a fact or consequence | Slogan endings on every section | Check the ledger |
| Understatement and omission | Hemingway | Lets the reader feel what is not said | State the fact; leave the adjective out | Telegraphic compression that drops grammar | Every piece |
| Controlled absurdity | Bulgakov | Names a situation that has become surreal | Describe the absurd fact flatly | Whimsy about ordinary failure | chellarams (Woland's banknotes) |
| Fragility test | Taleb | Shows where a structure breaks | Ask what a small shock does to the weakest link | Name-dropping "antifragile" | uber-nigeria-go-slow (Lindy effect) |
| Causal ladder | Aristotle | Separates material, efficient and final causes | List what changed, through which channel, for what end | Classification for its own sake | Check the ledger (Aristotle last quoted in uber-nigeria-go-slow) |
| Dry Nigerian specificity | House | Grounds a claim in a lived price or habit | A sachet, a generator, a danfo fare, with a date | Archetypes (uncle, agbada, Lagos traffic) | Every piece |
| Sentiment extremes | Jared Dillian, contrarian guide (2015) | Locates the crowd at its most certain | Show the bullish case at its most compelling and test it against cash | Contrarian for its own sake | Not yet |
| The verdict deferred to the evidence | Stop Slop | Removes the verdict sentence | Put the evidence first; let the claim follow as a clause | Leaving no claim at all | Every piece |
| Message title | Hemingway Slides | Puts the conclusion where executives read | Title is the finding with its number | Noun-stack titles | Decks |
| Table instead of sentence | Operations reporting | Removes number-heavy sentences | Any sentence with more than three numbers becomes a table row | Tables for two numbers | Reports |
| Planting and callback | Chekhov's gun, long-form craft | Makes the ending feel earned | Plant the opening image; return two or three times; resolve at the close | Callbacks every section | chellarams |

## Exemplar notes

- **The Economist Style Guide** (house library): Orwell's six rules; tone tests; preferred plain words; avoid imperative sentence openers aimed at the reader. Folded into `core/02-human-prose.md` section 4a.
- **SPE Style Guide** (2011): technical-paper conventions. Folded into `modules/phd.md` section 10.
- **Jared Dillian, contrarian guide** (2015): herds include smart people; the bullish argument is most compelling at the top; own what you recommend. Licensed text: quote sparingly with attribution.
