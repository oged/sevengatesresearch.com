### The answer before the sermon

NGX Group is becoming a better company faster than I expected.

Unfortunately, everybody else appears to have noticed.

The share closed at **₦149.90 on 14 September**, up from **₦130.10 on 7 September**, a gain of about **15.2% in one trading week**. The timing is not mysterious. Nigeria’s return to the FTSE Frontier universe takes effect on 21 September. On the morning this article was refreshed, Dangote launched the continent’s largest IPO, offering 4.1 billion refinery shares at ₦525 each and seeking roughly ₦2.15 trillion. The shovel seller has not merely found a gold rush. One of Africa’s largest miners has just arrived at the counter with a purchase order. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/facts-about-nigerias-dangote-oil-refinery-initial-public-offering-2026-09-14/) reports that the offer closes on 13 October and trading is expected in late November.

Those developments strengthen the strategic case for NGX. They do not improve the entry price.

My base SOTP remains approximately **₦105–₦120 per share**. The bull range is roughly **₦150–₦169**, depending on which share-count denominator ultimately proves correct. At ₦149.90, the market has walked to the front door of the bull case.

So the view changes slightly from **WATCH / HOLD** to **HOLD / WAIT**.

Existing holders with sensible position sizes can justify staying aboard. I would not initiate aggressively here. Below ₦110 the argument becomes more interesting. Around ₦95–₦100 I would become much more interested, assuming the operating thesis remains intact.

A great business can be worth owning. It does not follow that every price is an invitation.

## 1. The lady selling the shovels

There are three dependable characters in every gold rush.

There is the man who knows, with the confidence normally reserved for prophets and WhatsApp investment groups, that the gold is under *that* hill.

There is the man actually digging.

And somewhere nearby, considerably cleaner than both of them, is the person selling shovels.

The first may become a billionaire. He may also spend six years discovering granite.

The second may strike a vein. He may equally discover that geological optimism is not recognised by his bank as collateral.

The shovel seller has a simpler proposition: **cash first, archaeology afterwards.**

This is why the old picks-and-shovels investment metaphor survives. You do not have to know which prospector wins. You need enough people to keep prospecting.

Nigeria is suddenly doing quite a lot of prospecting.

The equity market has rerated. Domestic institutions have returned. Retail participation is widening. Settlement has moved to T+1. FTSE Russell will restore Nigeria to Frontier Market status from the open on **21 September 2026**, after concluding that no material settlement, operational or funding problems had emerged from T+1. S&P has Nigeria on a watch list for possible Frontier treatment in its 2027 country-classification review. [NGX Group’s statement](https://ngxgroup.com/ngx-group-ftse-russell-confirmation-reinforces-nigerias-capital-market-progress/) captures the opportunity reasonably well: international visibility now has to become deeper participation and more capital formation.

And today, 14 September, Dangote Refinery launched what Reuters describes as Africa’s largest IPO. Roughly 4.1 billion shares are being offered at ₦525, with proceeds intended in part to fund the next expansion phase. Whether one loves the valuation, hates it or requires several espressos before deciding, the exchange gets paid for the existence of the event.

So yes.

**NGX is the lady selling the shovels to Nigeria’s gold miners.**

But the metaphor understates the opportunity.

She sells the shovel. She charges admission to parts of the mine. She takes a fee when the gold changes hands. Through a **43.52% interest in CSCS**, she owns a large piece of the machinery that records who owns the gold after the trade. She would also quite like to sell you the map.

Selling shovels is a very good business.

Owning the map is better.

## 2. Huxley left an uncomfortable instruction manual

A sentence often passed around as Aldous Huxley says that *the past is the key to the future*.

I cannot verify that wording as Huxley. It therefore does not get to enter the article merely because it sounds expensive.

He wrote something better in **“A Case of Voluntary Ignorance”**, first published in *Esquire* in 1956 and later collected in 1959:

> **“That men do not learn very much from the lessons of history is the most important of all the lessons that history has to teach.”**

NGX is an unusually good company through which to test the proposition.

Go back to 2016.

Nigeria was in recession. Trading activity collapsed. Transaction fees fell 37%. Listing fees fell 41%. The old Nigerian Stock Exchange reported profit after tax of just **₦27.45 million**.

Not billion.

Million.

Then came 2017. The I&E FX window improved the ability of foreign portfolio investors to enter and exit the market. Total income rose 86%. Transaction fees jumped 130% to ₦3.6 billion. PAT went from ₦27.45 million to **₦3.79 billion**.

Management understood what had happened. The 2017 annual report said market-services revenues needed to become less correlated with market cycles and, importantly, global oil prices. [The report](https://doclib.ngxgroup.com/aboutus-site/Annual%20Reports/The%20Nigerian%20Stock%20Exchange%202017%20Annual%20Report.pdf) could almost have been written as a note to the 2026 board.

Nine years later, NGX has just produced a magnificent first half.

Revenue: **₦17.60 billion**, up 118%.

Operating profit: **₦10.62 billion**, up 155%.

Profit after tax: **₦10.36 billion**, up 146%.

Share of profit from equity-accounted investees: **₦4.14 billion**, up 130%, principally because of CSCS. [NGX Group H1 2026](https://ngxgroup.com/ngx-group-delivers-record-first-half-performance-announces-%E2%82%A61-30-interim-dividend/) is not merely a good result. It is an operating-leverage demonstration.

Transaction fees, however, were **₦13.34 billion**.

That is **75.8% of revenue**.

Market data plus technology income was only about **₦1.53 billion**, or roughly 8.7%.

The diagnosis in 2017 was correct.

The patient is richer.

The cure remains unfinished.

**Same examination paper. New school year.**

## 3. Ten years of peers: what actually compounded?

NGX Group itself cannot offer a ten-year listed-share record because it only listed on **15 October 2021**. Its predecessor can give us a decade of operating history. For listed performance, the more useful ten-year exercise is to study what the older exchange and financial-infrastructure businesses became.

Approximate price-only performance from September 2016 to September 2026 looks like this:

| Company | Approx. 2016 price | Sep 2026 price | Approx. multiple | Price CAGR | The lesson |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| **CME Group** | $107.55 | $275.54 | **2.6x** | **~9.9%** | Liquidity can itself become the product |
| **Intercontinental Exchange** | $55.96 | $157.40 | **2.8x** | **~10.9%** | Own the exchange, data and infrastructure around it |
| **LSEG** | ~2,800p | ~8,374p | **~3.0x** | **~11.6%** | The exchange can become the front door to a data company |
| **FICO** | $128.18 | $985.39 | **7.7x** | **~22.6%** | Own a standard embedded inside somebody else’s workflow |

This is not a total-return table. CME, ICE and LSEG have paid meaningful dividends, so the table understates their shareholder returns. FICO is not an exchange peer at all. It is included because its business model teaches something the pure exchange comparison misses.

The common thread is that the winners did not simply increase the number of trades on an old screen.

They embedded themselves deeper into financial workflows.

### CME: liquidity itself is the moat

CME reported **$6.52 billion of 2025 revenue**, of which $5.28 billion came from clearing and transaction fees and another $803 million from market data. At first glance that looks suspiciously like the transaction dependence I am criticising at NGX.

The difference is the quality of the liquidity pool.

If major institutions need to hedge US rates, SOFR, Treasuries, energy, metals or agriculture, established open interest and deep liquidity reduce execution cost. Lower execution cost attracts more participants. More participants deepen liquidity. Liquidity attracts liquidity.

That loop is difficult to reproduce with a glossy product-launch photograph.

The lesson for NGX is therefore not *launch more derivatives*.

It is **make a small number of derivatives genuinely necessary**.

Nigeria has obvious risk-transfer problems in FX, rates, equity indices and selected commodities. One liquid contract used by banks, asset managers, pension funds and corporates is worth more than twenty instruments whose most active day is the launch ceremony.

### ICE: sell the whole toolbox

ICE is perhaps the cleanest blueprint. Its 2025 net revenue was roughly **$9.9 billion**. Recurring revenue was about **$5.1 billion**, while transaction revenue was about **$4.9 billion**.

The exchange business remains excellent. But ICE also built fixed-income data, reference pricing, indices, connectivity and mortgage technology around the movement and financing of financial assets.

This is diversification with a mechanism.

Not diversification because somebody returned from a retreat with a slide saying **ADJACENCIES**.

### LSEG: eventually “stock exchange” became an incomplete description

LSEG now spans Data & Analytics, FTSE Russell, Risk Intelligence, Markets, clearing and institutional workflows. That is the future NGX should study most closely.

The deeper insight is not that LSEG bought Refinitiv. It is that the company learned that an exchange produces unusually valuable raw material: trusted data, identifiers, reference information, prices, benchmarks, connectivity and relationships with the institutions that move capital.

A trade happens once.

A data subscription wakes up every morning and invoices you again.

An index licence embeds itself inside a fund.

A pricing feed gets welded into a risk system.

A clearing relationship becomes infrastructure.

That is a very different revenue quality.

### FICO: own the definition

FICO is the stranger comparison and perhaps the most useful.

Its economic achievement was not simply inventing a credit score. It created a number that other institutions organised themselves around.

Banks use it. Consumers know it. Lenders design processes around it. Software recognises it. A measurement that becomes an industry standard is no longer merely information.

It becomes language.

NGX has smaller versions of this opportunity everywhere:

- indices and factor benchmarks;
- bond and money-market indices;
- corporate-action data;
- security identifiers;
- free-float and ownership records;
- machine-readable filings;
- issuer reference data;
- historical Nigerian fundamentals;
- market calendars and rulebooks that machines can consume.

The most attractive data product is not necessarily the one a customer admires.

It is the one whose disappearance ruins his Tuesday.

## 4. Does NGX actually have a moat?

Yes.

But moats are rarely uniform around the castle.

The strongest parts of NGX’s moat are the boring ones: regulatory status, institutional trust, issuer relationships, broker connectivity, market rules, liquidity concentration, surveillance, settlement and the economics attached to adjacent infrastructure such as CSCS.

You cannot recreate a credible national securities market because three programmers have discovered React and a venture capitalist has discovered the word *disruption*.

Legal title matters.

Settlement matters.

Market surveillance matters.

Clearing matters.

Regulatory recognition matters.

The weak parts of the moat sit closer to the user interface.

Generic dashboards can be copied. Elementary screening can be copied. Routine commentary can be copied. Basic charting can be copied. Much of what used to look like “financial technology” was, on inspection, simply a prettier route to the same data.

Which brings us to AI.

## 5. AI is coming for the screen, not the settlement

There is a version of the AI story in which artificial intelligence eats exchanges.

I do not buy it.

An AI agent can explain a trade. It cannot confer legal settlement finality.

It can analyse a prospectus. The issuer still needs somewhere to list.

It can model collateral. It does not become a recognised clearing institution because it has read the rulebook quickly.

The threat is in the information layer above the plumbing.

Suppose an institutional investor can ask an AI agent:

*Show me every Nigerian listed company with five years of positive dollar EPS growth, falling leverage, insider buying and an earnings yield above the market.*

The value no longer lies mainly in the interface through which somebody used to click six filters.

It lies in the quality, structure, rights, history and reliability of the underlying data.

That is why LSEG’s response to AI is so instructive. It is not trying to hide trusted data from AI. It is trying to make proprietary, licensed data available inside AI workflows. NGX should think the same way at Nigerian scale.

Do not defend yesterday’s screen.

**Own tomorrow’s source.**

NGX already sells real-time, end-of-day, reference and historical data through APIs and vendors. The opportunity is to move from “data available” to **Nigeria’s authoritative machine-readable capital-market record**.

Then let every serious terminal, fintech and AI system pay to ask it questions.

Huxley gives us a second line worth using. In *A Note on Dogma*, collected in *Proper Studies* in 1927, he wrote:

> **“Facts do not cease to exist because they are ignored.”**

The fact NGX cannot ignore is that AI will reduce the value of mediocre interfaces.

The equally important fact is that trustworthy proprietary data may become more valuable when machines can consume all of it.

## 6. CSCS may be the jewel hiding in the plumbing

NGX owns **43.52% of Central Securities Clearing System Plc**.

Trading receives the attention because trading has screens.

Post-trade infrastructure has fewer television adverts.

It also has an attractive habit of becoming deeply embedded.

NGX’s share of profit from equity-accounted investees reached **₦4.14 billion in H1 2026**, up 130%, with management saying the improvement was driven primarily by CSCS.

The next opportunity is not merely settlement.

It is collateral management, securities lending, repo infrastructure, clearing, margin, corporate actions and asset servicing. Each one pushes the group deeper into the operating machinery through which Nigerian securities move.

Nigeria’s successful transition to T+1 is strategically important for precisely this reason. FTSE Russell specifically reviewed the settlement process before confirming Frontier re-entry and said it had observed no material settlement, operational or funding problems. That is the kind of sentence market infrastructure earns slowly and monetises later.

Trust is boring until it becomes scarce.

## 7. CAPE enters the room carrying a bucket of cold water

The current earnings multiple does not look ridiculous.

Using trailing earnings data, NGX trades at roughly **26.5x TTM earnings** around ₦149.90. If one annualises the unusually strong H1 2026 PAT, the apparent run-rate multiple looks lower still.

That is precisely why CAPE is useful here.

I reconstructed a company-level cyclically adjusted P/E using ten complete financial years, 2016 through 2025. Historical group earnings were restated into current purchasing power using Nigerian CPI, then averaged. The resulting ten-year real earnings base is approximately **₦8.74 billion a year**.

Against the current market value, the result is:

- **~44.9x CAPE** using the exchange-reported 2.619 billion shares; and
- **~50.4x CAPE** using the potential 2.939 billion post-bonus denominator.

The publishing shorthand is therefore:

## **Seven Gates reconstructed CAPE: ~47x**

This is not a pristine Shiller CAPE. NGX Group itself was not listed for the full decade. The predecessor NSE operated under a different ownership structure. Demutualisation changed the corporate architecture. CSCS has become more economically important. Nigerian CPI has also been rebased.

So call it what it is: a **Seven Gates reconstructed CAPE**, a normalisation diagnostic rather than a vendor statistic.

But do not miss what the result is saying.

The enormous gap between roughly **26.5x trailing earnings** and roughly **47x ten-year real earnings** tells us exactly what the market believes.

### The market is pricing NGX as though the boom has become the business.

That may prove correct.

It is simply not something I would assume for free.

## 8. The denominator problem has not disappeared

Valuation is complicated by the share count.

At year-end 2025 NGX had roughly **2.205 billion issued shares**. Shareholders subsequently approved a **one-for-three bonus issue**. Full issuance would take the number to roughly **2.939 billion** shares.

Yet the NGX company profile currently reports **2.619 billion shares outstanding**. At ₦149.90 that gives an equity value of about **₦392.6 billion**. The full potential bonus-adjusted denominator gives approximately **₦440.6 billion**.

That is a difference of nearly ₦48 billion.

It moves CAPE by more than five turns.

It also changes every per-share SOTP.

Until the bonus capital structure is completely reconciled, the sensible approach is to show both denominators rather than bury the uncertainty beneath an EPS number printed to two decimal places.

Precision without a clean denominator is arithmetic wearing cufflinks.

A consolidated P/E is not enough because NGX contains businesses with different economics.

There is the core exchange and subsidiaries. There is the 43.52% interest in CSCS and other associates. There are liquid investment assets. There is clearing optionality. And there is a data and infrastructure franchise that may become much more valuable if management executes properly.

A week ago the valuation was demanding.

## 10. Dangote is wonderful for NGX. That does not make ₦150 cheap.

The Dangote Refinery IPO is almost comically well timed for the shovel-seller thesis.

Reuters reports that the company is offering 4.1 billion shares at ₦525 each, seeking roughly **₦2.15 trillion**. The offer has been marketed as a “people’s IPO”, with a deliberately low minimum purchase. Trading is expected to begin in late November. The proceeds are intended partly to finance an expansion from 700,000 barrels a day toward 1.4 million barrels a day.

Whatever one thinks of the refinery valuation, this is precisely the kind of transaction that makes an exchange more relevant.

It brings listing economics.

It brings trading economics.

It brings new retail accounts.

It brings market data consumption.

It raises international visibility.

It gives index providers something large to think about.

And if Dangote eventually brings more group companies to public markets, it may be the beginning of a pipeline rather than a single spectacular event.

But an IPO is revenue.

A sequence of important IPOs is a franchise.

A market infrastructure company that generates recurring data, clearing, benchmark and workflow revenue even when IPOs disappear for two years is a moat.

NGX has the first today.

It is progressing toward the second.

The third remains the prize.

## 11. What management should do next

NGX does not need to become LSEG in Nigerian costume.

It needs to steal the economic lessons without stealing the corporate furniture.

### Make recurring revenue a board-level obsession

Market data plus technology is still less than 10% of revenue in the latest half. I would like to see less transaction-sensitive revenue moving toward **25% to 35% of group revenue over five to seven years**.

Not because transaction revenue is bad.

Because recurring revenue makes the good years less necessary.

### Build the definitive Nigerian financial-data layer

Financial statements. Corporate actions. Security identifiers. Free float. Historical prices. Ownership where legally available. Indices. Bond data. Reference data. Issuer histories. Machine-readable filings.

Then distribute it everywhere serious Nigerian capital is analysed.

Bloomberg. Portfolio systems. Excel. Python. Fintechs. APIs. AI agents.

The customer should not need to visit an NGX webpage to consume NGX data.

### Build derivatives around pain, not product launches

FX. Rates. Equity indices. Selected commodities.

But liquidity first.

Announcements later.

A single contract that institutions repeatedly use to transfer risk is worth more than a catalogue of products whose primary market is conference photography.

### Use CSCS to deepen post-trade economics

Repo. Securities lending. Collateral. Clearing. Asset servicing. Corporate actions.

The ugly machinery tends to have beautiful switching costs.

### Own more benchmarks

FTSE Russell shows what happens when intellectual property becomes the number against which capital measures itself.

Nigeria has not exhausted the opportunity in domestic indices, factor products, fixed-income benchmarks and investable sector baskets.

### Enter private markets carefully

Nigeria has a large universe of important private companies. NGX should be economically relevant to high-quality companies before IPO day, not introduced to them for the first time when a prospectus arrives.

But empire building is the danger.

The lesson from ICE and LSEG is not *buy things*.

It is **own more of the workflow surrounding capital**.

Those instructions look annoyingly similar in PowerPoint.

They produce very different shareholder returns.

## 12. What could make this thesis wrong?

The strongest objection to the bullish story is hiding in plain sight: **NGX may be enjoying an exceptional cycle and mistaking it for a new business model.**

Transaction fees still dominate revenue. Current profits are benefiting from extraordinary market activity. Dangote is an unusually large listing. Frontier re-entry is a discrete catalyst. None of those conditions is guaranteed to repeat annually.

The bear case does not require Nigeria’s capital market to fail.

It merely requires activity to normalise before recurring revenues have become large enough to soften the fall.

Other risks matter too:

- a reversal in foreign-exchange liquidity or repatriation conditions;
- weaker equity-market turnover after the current enthusiasm fades;
- slower development of derivatives and securities lending;
- regulatory changes that alter fee economics;
- capital allocation mistakes in pursuit of an LSEG-like transformation;
- overpaying for technology or data acquisitions;
- disappointing monetisation of data despite impressive digital language;
- the share-count uncertainty resolving at the less favourable denominator.

What would make me more bullish at today’s price is also clear.

I want evidence that the earnings mix is changing.

Not another product announcement.

Not another market-cap record.

Not another excellent quarter driven mainly by transaction fees.

I want recurring data, indices, technology, clearing and post-trade income becoming large enough that a quiet Nigerian equity year no longer produces a quiet NGX income statement.

That would justify a different multiple.

# The Verdict

NGX Group owns a genuinely attractive asset.

Its regulatory position is valuable. Its operating leverage is formidable. CSCS gives it an unusually attractive interest in the post-trade plumbing. Nigeria’s improving market accessibility creates a structural tailwind. Dangote Refinery’s enormous IPO has arrived at exactly the moment one would choose if tasked with advertising the usefulness of an exchange.

And AI is more opportunity than existential threat, provided NGX understands what it actually owns.

The regulated exchange is difficult to disrupt.

The mediocre interface is not.

So the strategic objective should be to become the authoritative Nigerian data, benchmark and market-infrastructure layer that humans and machines both have to use.

The security is another matter.

At **₦149.90**, the market is already charging for a meaningful portion of that transformation.

The reconstructed **~47x CAPE** makes the expectation visible. The ordinary trailing multiple says NGX has become profitable. CAPE says investors believe those profits belong to a new regime.

Maybe they do.

Huxley’s warning is useful precisely because the company has seen this movie before. In 2017 management correctly identified transaction dependence as the weakness. Nine years later, transaction fees are again carrying most of the income statement.

The company has improved enormously.

The exam question has not disappeared.

My action at 14 September 2026 is therefore:

### **HOLD / WAIT at ₦149.90**

### **ACCUMULATE below roughly ₦110**

### **Materially more interested around ₦95–₦100**

I would revisit the upper end only when data, indices, clearing, securities lending and other recurring economics prove that the earnings mix has changed enough to deserve it.

The ultimate investment case is larger than today’s exchange.

The ideal NGX gets paid when a company raises capital.

When an investor trades.

When the trade settles.

When collateral moves.

When shares are borrowed.

When a fund licenses an index.

When a terminal requests data.

When an AI agent in London, Lagos or New York asks for the definitive Nigerian corporate-action record at three in the morning.

The lady should keep selling shovels.

But the truly wonderful business begins when **she owns the road to the mine, the weighing scale, the registry and the map**.

At ₦149.90, the market has noticed the shovel shop.

It has also started charging us for the railway.
