Verified attacks, fewer visible transits and higher oil prices now form one transmission chain through the Strait of Hormuz. For Nigeria, stronger crude receipts can help public revenue while dearer shipping, refined fuel and dollar funding raise costs elsewhere.

## 1. Seventeen tankers were struck in Hormuz this month

The Joint Maritime Information Center recorded four attacks in its latest 96-hour window and **17 tankers struck since 1 November**. Only three cargo-carrying vessels above 1,000 deadweight tonnes were visible on AIS in the strait from 22 to 25 November, although assisted and non-visible traffic continued.

Brent reached $96.85 early Friday, up 4.6% for the week but still nearly 18% lower for November. Nigeria gains if higher prices persist and export volumes hold. The offset is higher landing cost for fuel and industrial inputs, plus insurance and freight pressure on imports.

**Sources:** [UKMTO/JMIC, 26 November](https://www.ukmto.org/-/media/ukmto/products/jmic_advisory_update_117_26-nov.pdf); [Reuters, 27 November](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/global-markets-global-markets-2026-11-27/).

## 2. The CBN expanded licensed BDC access to dollar cash

The Central Bank temporarily raised each licensed bureau de change's weekly purchase ceiling from **$150,000 to $1 million** and allowed up to 100% of an approved purchase in cash. The previous cash ceiling was $37,500, making the new maximum 26.7 times larger.

Requests still pass through the FX BDC Purchase Tracker, and banks retain reporting duties. On 25 November, the official naira rate strengthened to ₦1,392.25 per dollar while the BDC rate weakened to ₦1,460. The directive gives the regulated channel more capacity, but it sets no public expiry date or aggregate supply target.

**Sources:** [CBN framework summary, updated 2026](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/AboutCBN/Reforms.html); [Proshare, 26 November](https://www.proshare.co/articles/nigeria-in-1min-economic-business-and-financial-market-headlines-261126?category=All+One+Min+News&classification=Read&menu=Business).

## 3. Dangote Refinery prepared for trading after heavy IPO demand

Dangote Group said refinery shares are expected to begin trading on the Nigerian Exchange on 1 December after approval of the allotment plan. Applications covered 11.15 billion shares, against 5.196 billion approved for distribution at ₦525 each. The allocation is worth about ₦2.73 trillion and involved 3.78 million subscribers.

The listing adds an unusually large operating asset to Nigeria's public market. It also concentrates exposure in one refinery whose earnings depend on utilisation, crude supply, product pricing, foreign exchange and maintenance. Oversubscription measures demand at the offer price. Trading will reveal how investors price those risks.

**Sources:** [Dangote Refinery, 25 November](https://refinery.dangote.com/dangote-refinery-targets-early-december-listing-after-secs-2bn-allotment/); [Reuters, 25 November](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/shares-dangote-oil-refinery-start-trading-nigeria-tuesday-group-vp-says-2026-11-25/).

## 4. The Bank of England put scale on AI's financing links

The Bank of England said hyperscaler bond issuance is set to exceed **$305 billion** in 2026, about 8% of global investment-grade issuance. It estimates potential exposure, including debt and off-balance-sheet commitments, at just above $4 trillion. Cross-investment, special-purpose vehicles and private credit obscure where risk ultimately sits.

Nigeria's exposure is mainly indirect. A reassessment of AI revenue could raise global credit spreads and reduce funding for emerging-market borrowers and African startups. Nigerian institutions need look-through data to assess the underlying concentration.

**Source:** [Bank of England Financial Stability Report, 26 November](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/financial-stability-report/2026/november-2026).

## 5. Nigeria promised support and an investigation after the NAF 931 loss

At a memorial for the 25 people killed in the 5 October NAF 931 aircraft accident, President Bola Tinubu approved a three-bedroom flat and ₦100 million for each bereaved family. The Air Force pledged a comprehensive and transparent investigation. The victims' remains have not been recovered.

Compensation addresses immediate family welfare. Public confidence will depend on a published account, corrective action and evidence that support reaches every family. The loss requires institutional learning as well as remembrance.

**Source:** [State House, 26 November](https://statehouse.gov.ng/president-tinubu-honours-25-victims-of-naf-931-aircraft-accident-donates-houses-n100m-to-each-of-the-deceased-families/).

## Three numbers worth remembering

- **17:** tankers struck in the Strait of Hormuz since 1 November.
- **$1 million:** the temporary weekly FX purchase ceiling for each licensed BDC.
- **11.15 billion:** Dangote Refinery shares requested in the IPO.

## What could change everything?

A sustained recovery in Hormuz traffic would reduce oil's risk premium. CBN disbursements and the retail exchange-rate gap will test the FX measure. Dangote's first sessions will establish price and liquidity. AI credit disclosure could reveal where concentrated losses would land.
