Nigeria has chosen a licensed, capital-intensive route for stablecoins. The new rules require local incorporation, sizeable paid-up capital and liquid reserves. They give banks and large fintechs a clearer path into digital settlement while making market entry difficult for smaller operators.

## 1. Nigeria has put stablecoins inside a formal payments regime

Central Bank of Nigeria regulations effective 5 November set minimum paid-up capital of **₦15 billion** for foreign-currency stablecoin issuers and ₦10 billion for naira issuers. Other covered non-security virtual-asset providers face a ₦1 billion floor, in addition to Securities and Exchange Commission capital where applicable. Existing operators have 180 days to apply.

Stablecoin reserves must cover the full par value of outstanding tokens plus at least 2% overcollateralisation. Redemption is due by the end of the next business day, algorithmic stablecoins are prohibited, and foreign-currency issuers must be incorporated in Nigeria. A stablecoin becomes systemically important after exceeding ₦500 billion in circulation for 30 consecutive days or five million Nigerian users.

The regime can improve reserve transparency and redemption certainty across remittances, merchant payments and digital-asset trading. It also concentrates access among well-capitalised firms. Customers still carry issuer and operational risk because the tokens are outside deposit insurance. Banks, fintechs and exchanges now have a 180-day window to decide whether the economics support a licence, local reserves and continuous compliance.

**Source:** [Central Bank of Nigeria regulations, dated 5 November](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2026/CCD/REGULATIONS%20FOR%20THE%20OPERATIONS%20OF%20NON-SECURITY%20VIRTUAL%20ASSETS%20IN%20NIGERIA.pdf).

## 2. South Africa's final declaration contains a 14-council gap

South Africa's electoral commission reported all 213 municipal councils finalised and declared on Sunday. The ANC led nationally with 30.89%, followed by the DA on 24.60%, MK on 9.61%, the EFF on 6.25% and ActionSA on 5.68%. Turnout reached **52%**, up 6.1 percentage points from 2021.

The declaration says 72 councils produced an outright majority and 127 are hung. Those figures account for 199 councils, leaving 14 unreconciled within the stated total of 213. It also orders a revote at one voting district after ballot papers were destroyed. Coalition talks will determine budgets, senior appointments and service-delivery programmes across most contested councils, but the commission should first correct or explain the published arithmetic. For Nigeria, the result shows the operating demands that follow when a dominant party loses local majorities: coalition agreements, voting discipline and transparent allocation of offices.

**Source:** [Electoral Commission of South Africa final declaration, 8 November](https://www.gov.za/news/speeches/chairperson-mosotho-moepya-official-declaration-2026-local-government-elections).

## 3. Nvidia's board has opened an independent investigation

Nvidia's independent directors have retained outside counsel after reports that chief executive Jensen Huang was targeted in a foreign-intelligence effort. The board said on Sunday that it currently had no reason to believe company secrets or national-security information were compromised. Huang is cooperating, and the board said it retained confidence in him.

The investigation turns personal exposure into a governance and national-security issue at the world's most important AI-chip supplier. Customers and governments will focus on access controls, disclosure and succession resilience. African cloud and data-centre plans that depend heavily on Nvidia hardware also inherit the policy and supplier-concentration risks surrounding the company.

**Source:** [Nvidia board statement, 8 November](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/Statement-from-NVIDIA-Board-of-Directors/default.aspx).

## 4. Oil has fallen as another US strike appears less likely

Brent traded near $98.35 early Monday after President Donald Trump said another strike on Iran was highly unlikely. Brent had fallen **16% last week**, its steepest weekly drop since the start of the COVID-19 pandemic, even as attacks on shipping and constraints around the Strait of Hormuz remained unresolved.

The retreat reduces the immediate crude-price windfall available to Nigeria. Shipping insurance and product freight can stay expensive while the benchmark falls, weakening the benefit for a country that exports crude and imports refined products and industrial inputs.

**Source:** [Reuters oil-market report, 8 November](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/oil-falls-open-trump-says-fresh-iran-attacks-unlikely-2026-11-08/).

## 5. COP31 opens with delivery as the test

COP31 opens in Antalya today and runs through 20 November. Nigeria's priorities include adaptation finance, resilient infrastructure and a transition strategy that preserves energy access. The practical test is whether negotiations produce funded projects, accessible financing terms and disbursement schedules. Announced totals without project-level commitments will leave implementation unchanged.

**Source:** [UN Framework Convention on Climate Change COP31 page, accessed 9 November](https://unfccc.int/cop31).

## Three numbers worth remembering

- **₦15 billion:** minimum paid-up capital for a foreign-currency stablecoin issuer in Nigeria.
- **52%:** South Africa's municipal-election turnout, up 6.1 percentage points from 2021.
- **16%:** Brent's fall last week, its steepest weekly decline since early 2020.

## What could change everything?

Early licence applications will show whether Nigeria's stablecoin market can support the new capital and reserve costs. A corrected council tally and signed coalition agreements will clarify South Africa's governing map. Evidence of compromised Nvidia information would sharply raise the investigation's significance. Renewed Gulf strikes or a sustained reopening of shipping routes could reverse oil's latest move. At COP31, published financing terms and named projects would provide the clearest evidence of delivery.
