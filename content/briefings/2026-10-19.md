Before Oyo can spend a disputed NLNG dividend on projects, someone must establish what was declared, remitted and allocated. Sunday's presidential rebuttal has made the disagreement more explicit. It has not supplied the complete reconciliation that citizens and state contractors need.

## 1. Abuja disputes Makinde's ₦250 billion-per-state claim

The Presidency on **18 October** rejected Governor Seyi Makinde's allegation that the Federal Government was withholding **₦250 billion for each state** until after the 2027 elections. Channels Television reports that Makinde said Oyo had already identified projects for the money.

The scale deserves scrutiny. Across 36 states, the claim implies **₦9 trillion**. Using the Presidency's stated **26.72%** state share of distributable Federation Account revenue implies a total pool of roughly **₦33.68 trillion**.

That calculation is conditional. It cannot establish whether arrears exist, what period a reconciliation covers, or whether the dispute concerns one declaration or accumulated remittances. The Presidency's single-payout argument needs to be matched against the precise claim and underlying records.

Seven Gates' view: publish the dividend declarations, remittance dates and FAAC allocation statements together. Neither a political allegation nor its denial should become a cash-budget assumption. Contractors need funded payment schedules; residents need to know which promised projects depend on disputed revenue. A published reconciliation could vindicate either account, or reveal that the parties are discussing different amounts and periods.

**Sources:** [State House, 18 October](https://statehouse.gov.ng/presidency-responds-to-governor-makindes-claim-on-nlng-dividends/) · [Channels Television, 18 October](https://www.channelstv.com/2026/10/18/nlng-dividend-delay-hurting-states-says-makinde/).

## 2. Egypt's defence council formally backs Saudi Arabia

Egypt's National Defense Council resolved on **18 October** to extend necessary and possible support for Saudi Arabia's security, stability and territorial integrity. Its statement also commits to intensified diplomacy and regional de-escalation.

This advances the institutional response beyond President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi's earlier offer. The published council statement does not specify deployed forces, an operational timetable or completed parliamentary authorisation. The Speaker's attendance at the meeting does not establish a parliamentary vote.

The strongest case for support is deterrence and better protection of civilian infrastructure. The opposing risk is a wider conflict that increases exposure for people and commercial routes. Nigerian businesses should keep those possibilities separate when reviewing shipments, staff travel and insurance. Ask carriers which route a particular cargo will use and what exclusions apply. Red Sea and Hormuz disruption require different alternatives; a general assurance of regional support cannot establish a delivery date.

**Source:** [Egyptian presidency, 18 October](https://www.presidency.eg/en/قسم-الأخبار/أخبار-رئاسية/news18102026/).

## 3. Higher oil accompanies sharply different regional equity moves

Reuters reports that Saudi Arabia's benchmark rose **0.8% on 18 October**, while Qatar fell **2%** and Egypt's blue-chip index declined **2.6%**. Brent had settled at **$109.86 a barrel on Friday, 16 October**, up **3.7%** that day. That is a dated futures settlement, not this morning's executable price.

These returns are not a controlled test of war exposure. Sector weights, local financing conditions and company-specific news also affect indices. They do show why a single regional label is inadequate for an investment decision.

For Nigeria, higher crude prices can improve export receipts when production and collections hold up. Imported products, freight and insurance can simultaneously become more expensive. State revenue, household transport costs and a listed producer's shareholder return therefore need separate calculations. Before increasing an earnings forecast, test realised selling prices, volumes, operating costs and the timing of cash collection.

**Source:** [Reuters, 18 October](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/oil-lifts-saudi-bourse-egypt-tumbles-regional-tensions-2026-10-18/).

## 4. Britain's Microsoft proposal makes software choice more concrete

The UK's Competition and Markets Authority proposed strategic market status for Microsoft's business software ecosystem on **16 October**. Possible future interventions concern bundling and interoperability, including the ability to combine competing software and AI services. This is a consultation, not a final designation or a finding of wrongdoing.

Responses are due by **5 p.m. on 13 November 2026**; the CMA says it will decide by **13 February 2027**.

Nigerian buyers gain no automatic legal entitlement from a UK proposal. They can still use the questions in procurement: can another AI assistant access permitted information, can security products be bought separately, and what does a tested migration cost? Integrated software can reduce administration and improve consistency. The comparison should price those benefits alongside switching constraints, data export and staff retraining, rather than assume that either bundling or unbundling is always cheaper.

**Sources:** [CMA announcement, 16 October](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-takes-first-steps-to-ensure-choice-in-business-software) · [CMA consultation, 16 October](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/proposed-decision-sms-investigation-into-microsofts-business-software-ecosystem).

## Three numbers worth remembering

- **₦9 trillion:** calculated aggregate state claim, not verified money received or owed.
- **$109.86:** Brent's 16 October futures settlement per barrel.
- **13 November:** deadline for responses to the CMA's proposed Microsoft designation.

## What could change everything?

A documented NLNG reconciliation would change state financing assumptions. Confirmed Egyptian authorisation and deployments would clarify military exposure. Verified shipping access would matter more for delivery schedules than diplomatic language. Final CMA requirements, followed by tested switching options, would turn software-policy debate into purchasing choices.
