Nigeria is attempting to test the machinery of a national election before voters must depend on it. Today's mock exercise covers accreditation and electronic result transmission across every state and the Federal Capital Territory. Its value will come from the failures it exposes and the fixes that follow.

## 1. INEC put accreditation and result transmission under national load

INEC's public results module lists **17,704 expected uploads for each of three mock contests**, or 53,112 across presidential, senatorial and House exercises. The Commission also reduced the maximum voters assigned to one BVAS device from 1,250 to 850, a 32% cut, and said it had introduced validation controls to stop inaccurate or over-voting results reaching IReV.

The rehearsal reaches 36 states and the FCT, 49 days before the 16 January presidential election. Coverage is useful; observable performance matters more. Completion rates, upload times, exception logs and remedial actions would show whether accreditation, connectivity, collation and security coordination worked under load.

**Sources:** [INEC announcement, 17 November](https://inecnigeria.org/press/inec-sets-28th-november-2026-for-nationwide-mock-accreditation-exercise); [INEC polling-unit list, 23 November](https://inecnigeria.org/press/list-of-polling-units-for-the-nationwide-mock-accreditation-scheduled-to-hold-on); [INEC results module, 28 November](https://cvr.inecnigeria.org/results).

## 2. The AU approved a two-stage Somalia security transition

The African Union Peace and Security Council endorsed AUSSOM transition phases running through December 2026 and January to December 2027. It requested a joint security assessment, including transferred locations, by September 2027 and sought extension of UN logistical support through 31 December 2027.

Five locations have already passed to Somali forces. The Council warned that a funding-led, uncoordinated withdrawal could let Al-Shabaab retake towns and supply routes. That makes sequencing a security variable, not an administrative detail. Nigeria contributes to AU peace and security policy; instability in the Horn also affects Red Sea trade, insurance and continental diplomatic capacity.

**Source:** [African Union Peace and Security Council communiqué, 27 November](https://www.peaceau.org/uploads/1380.comm-en.pdf).

## 3. US goods imports widened the trade deficit sharply

The US Census Bureau estimated October's goods deficit at **$149.5 billion**, up $14.4 billion, or 10.7%, from September. Imports rose $21.1 billion to $365.3 billion while exports increased $6.7 billion to $215.8 billion. Wholesale inventories also rose 0.9%.

The release complicates the signal from resilient demand: a larger import bill can support foreign producers, but it also adds to external imbalance as markets price a December Federal Reserve increase and the dollar heads toward a third monthly gain. For Nigeria, a stronger dollar and high US yields can raise external funding costs and put pressure on naira-priced imports even when US demand helps exporters.

**Sources:** [US Census Bureau, 27 November](https://www.census.gov/econ/indicators/2026/advance_report2610.pdf); [Reuters market report, 27 November](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/global-markets-global-markets-2026-11-27/).

## 4. Canada made subsidised compute an industrial-policy instrument

Canada added **C$700 million** to its AI Compute Access Fund, taking the programme to C$1 billion. A first phase has committed more than C$118 million to 89 companies; the expansion favours Canadian-owned, operated and governed capacity and supports a 500-megawatt sovereign-compute target.

The policy addresses a practical constraint: smaller firms cannot train and deploy competitive systems if compute access is scarce or unaffordable. Nigeria's AI plans face the same constraint with a harder power problem. Any local programme needs published access rules, power and connectivity costs, procurement terms and evidence that subsidies produce usable products rather than idle capacity.

**Source:** [Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada, 26 November](https://www.canada.ca/en/innovation-science-economic-development/news/2026/11/government-of-canada-boosts-the-ai-compute-access-fund-with-700-million-expansion.html).

## 5. New Zealand's final count removed two seats from National

New Zealand's Electoral Commission confirmed a 130-seat parliament after recounting ordinary votes and checking special votes. National fell from 42 election-night seats to 40, while the Greens rose from 16 to 17. National, ACT and New Zealand First now total 65 seats, one short of an outright majority.

The revised arithmetic raises the value of formal support arrangements and demonstrates why transparent post-election checks matter. The Commission published turnout, enrolment, special-vote and constituency-margin data, including two seats decided by fewer than 200 votes.

**Source:** [New Zealand Electoral Commission, 27 November](https://elections.nz/media-and-news/2026/2026-general-election-official-results).

## Three numbers worth remembering

- **53,112:** expected result uploads across INEC's three mock contests.
- **$149.5 billion:** the October US goods deficit.
- **C$700 million:** the new expansion of Canada's AI Compute Access Fund.

## What could change everything?

INEC's completion and exception data will determine whether the rehearsal produced evidence or ceremony. A failed extension of UN logistics could compress Somalia's transition. US rate expectations can shift with the next inflation and labour releases. Canada must convert compute support into deployed systems, while New Zealand's negotiations will determine who can command confidence.
