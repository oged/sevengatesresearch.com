Nigeria's central bank has chosen to keep restraint in place through the election and festive-spending season. Inflation has eased for five consecutive months, but the Monetary Policy Committee did not treat that progress as sufficient evidence for cheaper money.

## 1. The CBN kept the policy rate at 23%

The MPC retained the **23% Monetary Policy Rate**, its +50/-300 basis-point corridor and cash reserve requirements of 45% for deposit money banks, 16% for merchant banks and 75% for non-TSA public-sector deposits. Headline inflation fell to 14.06% in October from 14.63% in September. Core inflation was 10.78%, food inflation 19.13%, and gross reserves reached $54.02 billion, equal to 10.26 months of goods-and-services imports.

The hold keeps naira credit expensive and bank liquidity constrained. That supports disinflation and the currency, but limits the speed at which lower inflation reaches mortgages, working capital and investment. The CBN explicitly identified election-related and festive spending as upside risks. It also welcomed its new agreement with NNPC on export proceeds and energy financing. NNPC says working groups and implementation timelines exist, but has not published flow targets or reporting dates. The next MPC meeting is in February 2027, leaving three months for inflation and FX-market evidence to accumulate.

**Sources:** [Central Bank of Nigeria, 24 November](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2026/CCD/MPC%20Communique%20No%20165%20of%20November%202026.pdf); [NNPC Limited, 19 November](https://www.nnpcgroup.com/insights/press-release-nnpc-cbn-sign-deal-to-enhance-energy-financing).

## 2. The EU proposed removing about 60 Russian banks from SWIFT

The European Commission presented a 22nd sanctions package that would extend transaction bans and SWIFT removal to about 60 Russian banks, list more than 70 additional shadow-fleet vessels, restrict Russian-insured tankers and widen the ban on European energy services to electricity and refinery work. A new task force would target crypto and payment networks used to evade sanctions.

These are proposals, not completed measures. Adoption still depends on EU member states. If approved, the package would raise counterparty, insurance and payment-screening demands beyond Europe. Nigerian banks, traders and energy companies would need to check vessels, beneficial owners and settlement routes against the final legal text, particularly where Russian-linked shipping or third-country intermediaries are involved.

**Source:** [European External Action Service, 24 November](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/delegations/ukraine/statement-president-von-der-leyen-22nd-package-sanctions-against-russia_en).

## 3. Markets priced tariffs before the tariff report appeared

MSCI's Asia-Pacific index excluding Japan fell 3.2%. South Korea dropped as much as 6.4% and Taiwan 8.6% after reports that Washington could impose new duties following a Section 301 investigation into excess industrial capacity across 16 trading partners. At the London cut-off, USTR had published neither the report nor new rates.

The investigation is real; the remedy remains unconfirmed. USTR formally opened it in March against China, the EU and 14 other economies. A broader tariff cycle would affect Nigerian import prices for machinery, electronics and solar equipment, while weaker global risk appetite can raise dollar-funding costs. Trade diverted from the United States could reduce some import prices, but only after product-level duties and supplier responses are known.

**Sources:** [Reuters, 25 November](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/global-markets-global-markets-2026-11-25/); [Reuters, 24 November](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/trump-administration-set-unveil-new-tariffs-after-probe-on-excess-factory-2026-11-24/); [USTR, 11 March](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/march/ustr-initiates-section-301-investigations-relating-structural-excess-capacity-and-production).

## 4. Google moved the AI test from one answer to repeated reliability

Google DeepMind launched Humanity's First Exam, a benchmark of 544 elementary text and image prompts across 20 capabilities. It tests whether models answer straightforward questions and small variations consistently, rather than whether they can solve a difficult task once.

That is closer to the procurement problem faced by banks, telecoms and public agencies. A model used for Nigerian forms, customer service or compliance must produce dependable results across repeated cases, local documents and imperfect inputs. The benchmark is company-led and newly released, so independent results and local-language tests matter more than the launch claim.

**Source:** [Google DeepMind, 24 November](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/humanitys-first-exam/).

## Three numbers worth remembering

- **23%:** Nigeria's retained Monetary Policy Rate.
- **About 60:** Russian banks the EU proposal would remove from SWIFT.
- **544:** prompts in Google's new reliability benchmark.

## What could change everything?

A sustained fall in monthly inflation and stable FX liquidity could open the way to a February rate cut. EU unanimity would turn the sanctions proposal into operating law. The USTR report will determine whether markets priced the correct countries, products and rates. Independent HFE results will show whether repeated reliability improves outside Google's test design.
