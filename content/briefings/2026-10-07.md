The first duty this morning is correction. Yesterday's brief reported that 32 people died in the Nigerian Air Force crash, using the figure initially issued by government officials. The Air Force has now reviewed the manifest and says 25 people were aboard. Seven Gates has corrected the permanent edition and recorded the change. A smaller toll is not a smaller tragedy. It is also not an explanation for how two official counts differed by seven.

## 1. The Air Force says 25 died, not 32

The revised manifest contains **20 passengers and five crew members**. There were no survivors. The earlier figure, 25 passengers and seven crew, came from the Aviation Minister and the Defence Ministry on 5 October. Officials have not yet explained the discrepancy.

That explanation belongs beside the crash investigation, not beneath it. Reliable manifests determine who rescuers seek, which families receive information and what investigators reconstruct. The inquiry still needs maintenance, crew, weather, communications and flight-data evidence. It now also needs a short account of why the first public record was wrong.

**Sources:** [Associated Press, 6 October](https://apnews.com/article/nigeria-plane-crash-ondo-air-force-7f2f154f795d4bf66d33ba3d309d2f4c) · [Premium Times, 6 October](https://www.premiumtimesng.com/news/headlines/914791-updated-no-survivors-as-nigeria-releases-names-of-crew-members-passengers-killed-in-crashed-aircraft.html).

## 2. Kenya has confirmed its first imported Ebola case

A Kenyan citizen who had lived in the Democratic Republic of Congo travelled by road to Kampala and flew to Nairobi on 3 October. He died on 5 October. Kenya confirmed Bundibugyo Ebola the next day. Eight relatives and 21 health workers are quarantined, while officials are tracing 23 passengers and four crew from his flight.

The patient passed airport screening, possibly because medication masked his symptoms. That is the operational lesson: temperature checks cannot carry the whole defence. Travel history, clinical suspicion, rapid isolation, laboratory capacity and contact tracing matter more once an outbreak crosses several transport modes. Nigeria should review those layers now, especially for arrivals routed through East African hubs.

**Sources:** [Kenya News Agency, 6 October](https://www.kenyanews.go.ke/kenya-confirms-first-imported-ebola-case-patient-dies/) · [Reuters, 6 October](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/kenya-confirms-first-ebola-case-imported-congo-patient-has-died-2026-10-06/).

## 3. Africa's growth forecast improved, but so did its inflation forecast

The World Bank raised projected Sub-Saharan African growth for 2026 to **4.3%**, 0.3 percentage points above its April forecast and up from 4.1% in 2025. Nigeria is among the countries upgraded. Yet median regional inflation is expected to rise from 3.7% to **5.5%**, while public debt remains around 57% of GDP.

The chart explains why macroeconomic improvement may not translate quickly into household relief. The Bank wants practical, low-bandwidth AI for farms, schools, clinics and small firms, not an expensive race to train frontier systems. Nigeria already has concentrated AI activity. Its constraint is the supporting stack: reliable electricity, affordable connectivity, skills, usable data, compute and rules that permit adoption without weakening privacy.

**Source:** [World Bank, 6 October](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2026/10/06/economic-growth-in-sub-saharan-africa-gains-momentum-despite-global-uncertainty).

## 4. Wall Street reached records with oil still near $100

The S&P 500 gained 0.6% and the Nasdaq 0.45% to record closes on 6 October. Brent settled near **$100.60** a barrel. The US 10-year Treasury yield eased to 5.28%, still an exceptionally expensive reference rate, while expectations of an October Federal Reserve increase fell to 19% from roughly 50% a week earlier.

This is relief, not cheap money. Consensus expects 27% S&P 500 earnings growth, with more than half linked to AI infrastructure beneficiaries. Nigeria's oil receipts benefit from a three-digit barrel, but refinancing competes with a 5.28% Treasury yield before sovereign risk is added. Both numbers can support Nigerian revenue and raise Nigeria's cost of capital at the same time.

**Source:** [Reuters global markets, 6 October](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/global-markets-global-markets-2026-10-06/).

## 5. Mistral has put a European trillion-parameter model into preview

Mistral launched Large 4 as a public API preview: one trillion total parameters, 49 billion active parameters and training on 3,800 Nvidia Grace Blackwell GPUs in Europe. The company says model weights will be released by month-end. Its performance claims are partly internal and need independent replication.

The strategically important part is deployment control. Mistral is selling open weights, private operation and European legal jurisdiction as enterprise features. For Nigeria, "sovereign AI" should mean the practical ability to run useful models over sensitive local data, not merely hosting a launch event. Power, procurement, security testing and technical talent decide whether that promise is real.

**Source:** [Mistral AI, 6 October](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-large-4/).

## Three numbers worth remembering

- **25:** people on the Air Force's revised crash manifest.
- **5.5%:** World Bank forecast for median Sub-Saharan African inflation in 2026.
- **5.28%:** US 10-year Treasury yield at Tuesday's market close.

## What could change everything?

Any secondary Ebola transmission in Kenya would turn an imported case into a wider regional test. A published Air Force manifest reconciliation and evidence-led crash report would restore confidence in the official record. For markets, an oil-price break below $100 accompanied by lower long-term yields would ease two pressures together. For Africa's improved forecast, the real confirmation is faster per-capita income and job creation, not the regional headline alone.
